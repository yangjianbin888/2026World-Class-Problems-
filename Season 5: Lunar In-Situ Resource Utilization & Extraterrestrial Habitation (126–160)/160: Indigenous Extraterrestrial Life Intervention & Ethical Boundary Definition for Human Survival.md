**New-Route Core Solution**

Core architecture: A **tiered ethical framework** built on three conceptual pillars, reconciling planetary protection with human survival through operational gradation:

**Pillar A – Life Value Recognition (Beyond Instrumentalism):** Extraterrestrial life is recognized as having:
- **Epistemic value** (source of knowledge about biology, evolution, and the universe)—the current COSPAR foundation.
- **Intrinsic value** (value in itself, independent of human utility)—requires stronger protection, as argued in microbial ethics literature .
- **Instrumental value for future generations** (potential resource for medicine, biotechnology, or ecosystem services).
- **Cultural/symbolic value** (significance to human meaning and cosmic perspective).

This multi-value recognition shifts the ethical calculus from "protect only for science" to "protect proportionally to value-weighted risk."

**Pillar B – Graded Intervention Scale (Operations):** Interventions are classified on a 5‑level scale with escalating ethical scrutiny:

- **Level 0 – Non-Contact Observation:** Remote sensing, flybys, orbital observation. No physical contact. Ethically unproblematic; baseline scientific activity.

- **Level 1 – Passive Study:** Probes that land or sample but do not intentionally introduce Earth organisms (Category IVb/C for life‑seeking missions ). Minimal contamination risk; COSPAR bioburden limits apply.

- **Level 2 – Minimal Intervention:** Activities that introduce Earth organisms or materials to a target body, but in a manner that is localised, reversible, or transparent. E.g., sterile human outposts with waste containment, microbiome-controlled habitats. Requires comprehensive risk assessment.

- **Level 3 – Active Manipulation:** Deliberate introduction of Earth life for terraforming, agriculture, or ecosystem engineering. E.g., introducing nitrogen-fixing bacteria to Mars soil; genetically modifying crops for Martian regolith. Acceptable only if: (a) no indigenous life exists at the site (Type 0 only), or (b) indigenous life is microbial but the target region has been sterilised and containment is maintained.

- **Level 4 – Directed Panspermia:** Intentional seeding of lifeless worlds with Earth life to initiate new biospheres. As scholars have noted, such experiments raise unique ethical questions that require decisions about principled opposition or permissible action based on confidence in lifeless status . Permissible only for Type 0 worlds with 99.9% confidence in absence of indigenous life, and only under international oversight.

**Pillar C – Contextual Boundary Definition (Rules of Engagement):** The boundary between permissible and prohibited intervention is defined by a **three‑condition test**:

1. **The Knowledge Imperative Test:** Does the intervention generate knowledge that substantially outweighs the risk of harm? (Applied at Level 2–3).

2. **The Irreversibility Test:** Is the intervention reversible? If irreversible (e.g., directed panspermia or terraforming), requires higher scrutiny and international consensus.

3. **The Human Survival Necessity Test:** Is the intervention necessary for human survival or permanent settlement (per the "Principle of Humanity" )? If yes, intervention may be justified even if it conflicts with strict planetary protection—but only under conditional endorsement (see Implementation Path).
