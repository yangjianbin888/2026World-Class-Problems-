The stellarator’s inherent advantage—steady-state operation without disruptive current-driven instabilities—has historically been offset by neoclassical transport losses 1–2 orders of magnitude above tokamak baselines. The 60-point baseline—minimizing the effective ripple ε_eff—has reached a hard physical limit. This is not a tuning issue—it is a structural gap in the optimization metric. With ε_eff minimized, **the remaining parameter space holds no further leverage**. The 60-point baseline has exhausted all degrees of freedom that matter for its own figure of merit; further iteration yields diminishing returns and risks degrading MHD stability or coil complexity.
