**Abstract**
REBCO (Rare-Earth Barium Copper Oxide) coated conductors are the only practical path to 20T+ superconducting magnets for fusion and high-field research. However, the 60-point baseline is severely limited by two intertwined fatal flaws: (1) **Slow quench propagation** due to the high specific heat at 4.2-20K and low normal zone propagation velocity (NZPV, ~5-10 m/s), making quench detection and protection extremely difficult. (2) **Mechanical stress** from Lorentz forces exceeding 400 MPa, which degrades the conductor's critical current (Ic) and can cause delamination. The current baseline relies on "brute force" copper stabilizers and "hot-spot" heaters, but these are palliative, not curative. This roadmap proposes a **hybrid protection system** combining (a) **distributed, high-speed quench detection** via voltage taps and optical fibers, (b) **active protection** using a fast-discharge and a novel "quench-bypass" circuit, and (c) **structural stabilization** via a stainless steel or Incoloy support structure. The target is to achieve a 20T magnet with a quench detection time <50 ms and a hot-spot temperature <150K, enabling safe, stable operation.

**1. Pain Point Definition (Why)**
- **Slow Quench Propagation:** REBCO tapes have a high specific heat and low thermal conductivity. A local hot spot (quench) does not spread quickly. This means the energy of the entire 20T magnet (hundreds of MJ) is concentrated into a small region, leading to burnout.
- **Delamination:** The REBCO tape is a multi-layer structure (substrate, buffer layers, REBCO, and copper stabilizer). Under high Lorentz forces (>400 MPa), the shear stress between layers can exceed the weak bonding strength, leading to delamination and a sudden, catastrophic loss of superconductivity.
- **High Stored Energy:** A 20T magnet stores a tremendous amount of energy (~200-500 MJ). If this energy is dumped into a normal zone, the resulting temperature rise can melt the conductor.

**2. The 60-Point Ceiling (Old Route)**
The 60-point baseline uses a thick, high-conductivity copper stabilizer to "buy time" for quench detection. It also uses resistive "dump" heaters to spread the quench. However, this approach is a "Band-Aid": the copper stabilizer does not solve the problem; it only makes the magnet larger, more expensive, and more difficult to cool. *The 60-point ceiling has exhausted all headroom for parameter tuning – further adjustments only reduce efficiency, and any significant improvement requires a fundamental hardware change.*

**3. The Breakthrough Solution (What)**
**Core Concept:** A triple-tiered protection system:
1.  **Advanced Detection:** A system of distributed voltage taps (with a <1 ms response time) and distributed optical fiber (Bragg grating) sensors for temperature and strain measurement, providing early warning of a quench.
2.  **Active Protection:** A novel "quench-bypass" circuit. When a quench is detected, the circuit rapidly (in <10 ms) redirects the current from the quenched coil to a bypass coil (a secondary, uncoupled coil) via a high-speed solid-state switch (e.g., an IGBT). This limits the current in the quenched area, drastically reducing the hot-spot temperature.
3.  **Structural Reinforcement:** A stainless steel (e.g., 316L) or Incoloy (e.g., 825) support structure to contain the Lorentz forces, preventing delamination. The support structure is designed to be "pre-stressed" to reduce the peak stress on the REBCO tape.

**Parameter Benchmarking:**
- **Quench Detection Time:** Baseline 50-100 ms → Target <10 ms.
- **Hot-Spot Temperature:** Baseline >250K (conductor melt risk) → Target <150K.
- **Magnet B-Field:** Baseline 15T → Target 20T.
- **Max Stress (Tape):** Baseline 400 MPa → Target <300 MPa (via pre-stressing).
- **Magnet Volume:** Baseline Large → Target Compact (20% smaller).

**Supply Chain Anchoring (Standard-Driven):**
- **REBCO Tape:** SuperPower or SuNAM standard tapes (or equivalent). Need Ic > 500 A (at 77K, self-field) and a width of 4-6 mm.
- **Structural Steel:** Incoloy 825 or 316L stainless steel. Standard: ASTM B425 or A240.
- **Solid-State Switch:** High-power IGBTs, capable of switching 1 kA at 1 kV in <1 µs. Standard: IEC 60747.
- **Optical Fiber:** Bragg grating sensors for strain and temperature. Standard: ITU-T G.652.

**4. Implementation Path (How)**
- **Step A:** Develop and test the optical fiber detection system on a small REBCO coil (2T). *Acceptance Criteria:* Detect a temperature rise of <1K and a strain of <1 microstrain with a response time of <1 ms.
- **Step B:** Build a prototype of the "quench-bypass" circuit. *Acceptance Criteria:* Demonstrate a current interruption and bypass within <10 ms.
- **Step C:** Design and construct a full-scale 20T magnet with the integrated protection system. *Acceptance Criteria:* Achieve a stable 20T field (run for >1000 hours) with a hot-spot temperature of <150K during a simulated quench.

**摘要**
REBCO（稀土钡铜氧化物）涂层导体是实现20T+超导磁体用于聚变和高场研究的唯一实用路径。但其60分基线受到两个相互交织的致命缺陷的严重限制：（1）由于4.2-20K下高比热和低正常区传播速度（NZPV，约5-10 m/s）导致的**慢失超传播**，使失超检测和保护极为困难。（2）洛伦兹力超过400 MPa引起的**机械应力**，会降低导体的临界电流（Ic）并可能导致分层。当前基线依赖“蛮力”铜稳定器和“热点”加热器，但这些都是治标不治本。本路线图提出一种**混合保护系统**，结合（a）通过电压抽头和光纤的**分布式高速失超检测**，（b）使用快速放电和新型“失超旁路”电路的**主动保护**，以及（c）通过不锈钢或Incoloy支撑结构的**结构稳定化**。目标是在失超检测时间<50 ms和热点温度<150K下实现20T磁体，确保安全稳定运行。

**1. 痛点定义**
- **慢失超传播：** REBCO带具有高比热和低热导率。局部热点（失超）不会迅速扩展。这意味着整个20T磁体的能量（数百MJ）集中在局部区域，导致烧毁。
- **分层：** REBCO带是多层结构（基底、缓冲层、REBCO和铜稳定器）。在高洛伦兹力（>400 MPa）下，层间剪切应力可能超过弱结合强度，导致分层和突然的灾难性超导电性丧失。
- **高储能：** 20T磁体存储大量能量（约200-500 MJ）。如果这些能量倾泻到正常区，产生的温升可能熔化导体。

**2. 旧路线天花板（60分基线）**
旧的60分基线使用厚的高导电铜稳定器为失超检测“争取时间”。它还使用电阻“卸流”加热器来传播失超。然而，这种方法是一种“创可贴”：铜稳定器不能解决问题，只会使磁体更大、更昂贵、更难冷却。*旧路线的60分已经用完了所有可调参数的自由度——再调就是降效率，再改就是换设备。它的上限不是技术限制，是物理限制。*

**3. 破局方案**
**核心架构：** 三层保护系统：
1.  **先进检测：** 分布式电压抽头（响应时间<1 ms）和分布式光纤（布拉格光栅）温度/应变传感器系统，提供失超早期预警。
2.  **主动保护：** 新型“失超旁路”电路。当检测到失超时，电路通过高速固态开关（如IGBT）在<10 ms内将电流从失超线圈转移到旁路线圈（次级非耦合线圈）。这限制了失超区域的电流，大幅降低热点温度。
3.  **结构加固：** 不锈钢（如316L）或Incoloy（如825）支撑结构以容纳洛伦兹力，防止分层。支撑结构设计为“预加应力”以降低REBCO带的峰值应力。
