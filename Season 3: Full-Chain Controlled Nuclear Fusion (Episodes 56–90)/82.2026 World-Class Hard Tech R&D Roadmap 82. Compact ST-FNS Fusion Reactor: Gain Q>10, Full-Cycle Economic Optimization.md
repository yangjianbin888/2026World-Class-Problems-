**Abstract**
This roadmap addresses the core challenge of designing a compact Spherical Tokamak (ST) Fusion Neutron Source (FNS) that achieves an energy gain Q > 10 while maintaining economic viability over its full life cycle. The current 60-point baseline is defined by conventional tokamak FNS designs, which achieve Q~1 with large major radii (R₀ > 2.0 m) and high construction and operational costs. This 90-point solution leverages the inherent advantages of the ST configuration—high beta, high bootstrap fraction, and reduced size—to achieve Q > 10 in a device with R₀ ~ 0.5-0.8 m . The economic optimization is anchored in life-cycle cost modelling, design simplification, and the use of commercially available off-the-shelf (COTS) components, resulting in a projected total economics factor of 2 better than conventional designs . Key enablers include: (1) high bootstrap current fraction (f_BS > 50%) for reduced current drive power; (2) modest heating power (< 20 MW) to minimize recirculating power; and (3) vertical maintenance and modular design to reduce operational and decommissioning costs . This solution is based on demonstratable physics (already achieved in present-day STs) and standard materials.

**1. Pain Point Definition (Why)**
The 60-point baseline for fusion neutron sources is defined by conventional, medium-to-large aspect ratio tokamaks. These devices, such as the proposed DEMO-FNS (R₀ = 2.75 m), require high auxiliary heating power (30-50 MW) to achieve a modest gain of Q~1-2 . This manifests in three critical failure modes:
- **Failure Mode 1: High Recirculating Power.** To sustain the plasma current and achieve a net power gain, conventional tokamaks require significant external current drive and heating. The high toroidal magnetic field and large plasma volume demand large-scale, expensive magnet and power supply systems. For a Q~1 system, a significant fraction of the gross electrical output is recirculated back into the device to sustain the reaction .
- **Failure Mode 2: High Capital and Operational Costs.** Conventional designs, due to their size and complexity (e.g., large superconducting coils, complex remote handling), have high capital costs (estimated >$1B) and high tritium consumption (>700g/year) . The cost per neutron is too high for economic industrial applications like transmutation or medical isotope production. The total economics for a conventional FNS is significantly worse than for an ST option .
- **Failure Mode 3: Scalability to Pilot Plant.** The 60-point baseline is a dead-end for a compact, economically viable fusion power plant. The engineering and physics parameters of a conventional tokamak are not easily scalable to a smaller, cheaper, modular power source. The high aspect ratio and low beta limit the achievable fusion power density.

**Old Route Ceiling (60-point Baseline)**
The 60-point baseline for a fusion neutron source is represented by conventional tokamak designs with major radii > 2m, auxiliary heating power of 30-50 MW, and a fusion power of 40-50 MW. This yields a gain of Q~1 and a neutron wall loading of ~0.2 MW/m². The cost for such a facility is in the billions and requires a large tritium inventory . The old route's 60 points have used up all the adjustable parameter degrees of freedom for cost reduction. Any further performance improvement requires increasing the size of the device, which locks in the high cost. Its limit is not a technological limitation; it is a physical and economic one.

**2. Breakthrough Solution (What)**
**Core Architecture:** A compact Spherical Tokamak (ST) FNS with major radius R₀ ~ 0.5-0.8 m, utilizing high bootstrap fraction and neutral beam injection (NBI) for steady-state, non-inductive current drive. The design is optimized for a fusion neutron yield of 1-10 MW with low tritium consumption (< 50g/year), low capital cost (target < $200M), and high availability, using copper water-cooled toroidal field coils and a modular vertical maintenance scheme .

**Parametric Benchmark:**
- **Energy Gain (Q):** Baseline: Q~1 (DEMO-FNS conventional design) → **Solution: Q > 10** is targeted by optimizing heating power and plasma confinement in a smaller, more efficient plasma .
- **Major Radius (Device Size):** Baseline: R₀ = 2.75 m (CT design) → **Solution: R₀ = 0.5 - 1.3 m** (ST design), significantly reducing volume and cost .
- **Tritium Consumption:** Baseline: > 700 g/year → **Solution: < 50 g/year** for a compact ST, drastically reducing operational cost and inventory handling .
- **Auxiliary Heating Power:** Baseline: 36-60 MW → **Solution: < 15-20 MW** total heating and current drive power .
- **Current Drive & Bootstrap Fraction:** Baseline: f_BS ~ 50% → **Solution: f_BS > 50%**, minimizing the external power needed for current drive .
- **Total Economics (Projected Cost):** Baseline: Conventional tokamak FNS defined as 1x → **Solution: ST-FNS economics factor of 2x better** than a comparable CT device .

**Supply Chain Anchoring (COTS Focus):**
- **Toroidal Field (TF) Magnet System:** **Standard:** Copper alloy single-turn, water-cooled coils. **Performance Standard:** Capable of carrying up to 250 kA current with a peak magnetic field of ~1.5 T. This is a standard material used in existing ST devices (NSTX, MAST) .
- **Heating & Current Drive System:** **Standard:** Neutral Beam Injectors (NBI) with positive ion sources (130 keV). **Performance Standard:** Must deliver 5-15 MW of power. This is a commercially available technology .
- **Plasma-Facing Components:** **Standard:** Water-cooled tungsten or carbon-fiber composite (CFC) tiles. **Performance Standard:** Withstand steady-state heat fluxes up to 6-10 MW/m². These are standard materials for high heat flux environments .
- **Vacuum Vessel:** **Standard:** 316L austenitic stainless steel or CuCrZr alloy. **Performance Standard:** Must be a thin-wall, water-cooled structure capable of withstanding a heat load of up to 1.5-6 MW/m² .

- **Acceptance Criteria (Quantified):**

**Step C: Demonstrate High-Gain (Q>10), Steady-State Operation.**

    - **Achieve a steady-state fusion power output of 1-10 MW**.

    - **Demonstrate long-pulse operation** with the heat exhaust system (divertor and first wall) functioning within design limits. The heat flux on the divertor targets must be manageable (<6-10 MW/m²) .

**摘要**
本路线图旨在解决紧凑型球形托卡马克（ST）聚变中子源（FNS）实现能量增益Q > 10并同时保证全生命周期经济可行性的核心难题。当前60分基线由传统托卡马克FNS设计定义，这些设计在大半径（R₀ > 2.0 m）条件下仅达到Q~1，建造成本和运行成本高昂。本90分方案利用ST构型的固有优势——高比压、高自举份额和小尺寸——在R₀ ~ 0.5-0.8 m的装置中实现Q > 10 。经济优化锚定于全生命周期成本建模、设计简化和商用现货部件的使用，预计总经济性比传统设计优2倍 。关键使能技术包括：(1) 高自举电流份额（f_BS > 50%）以减少驱动功率；(2) 适中的加热功率（< 20 MW）以最小化再循环功率；(3) 垂直维护和模块化设计以降低运行和退役成本 。本方案基于已可演示的物理（在当前ST中已实现）和标准材料。

**1. 痛点定义**
聚变中子源的60分基线由中到大半径的传统托卡马克定义。这些装置，如提议的DEMO-FNS (R₀ = 2.75 m)，需要高辅助加热功率（30-50 MW）来实现Q~1-2的适度增益 。具体表现为三个关键失效模式：
- **失效模式1：高再循环功率。** 为维持等离子体电流并实现净功率增益，传统托卡马克需要大量外部电流驱动和加热。高环向磁场和大等离子体体积需要大规模、昂贵的磁体和电源系统。对于Q~1系统，相当一部分总电输出被再循环回装置以维持反应 。
- **失效模式2：高资本和运行成本。** 传统设计因其尺寸和复杂性（如大型超导线圈、复杂远程操控），具有高资本成本（估计>10亿美元）和高氚消耗（>700克/年）。每个中子的成本对于嬗变或医用同位素生产等经济工业应用而言过高。传统FNS的总经济性明显差于ST方案 。
- **失效模式3：向示范堆的可扩展性。** 60分基线对于紧凑、经济可行的聚变电厂是死胡同。传统托卡马克的工程和物理参数不易扩展到更小、更便宜、模块化的电源。高纵横比和低比压限制了可实现的聚变功率密度。

**旧路线天花板（60分基线）**
聚变中子源的60分基线以传统托卡马克设计为代表，其大半径>2m，辅助加热功率30-50 MW，聚变功率40-50 MW。这产生Q~1的增益和中子壁负载~0.2 MW/m²。这类设施的成本达数十亿美元，并需要大量氚库存 。旧路线的60分，已经用完了所有可调参数的自由度来降低成本。任何进一步的性能提升都需要增加装置尺寸，这锁定了高成本。它的上限不是技术限制，是物理和经济限制。
