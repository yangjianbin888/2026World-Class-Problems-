**Abstract**
Reduced Activation Ferritic/Martensitic (RAFM) steels (e.g., EUROFER97, CLAM, F82H) are the leading candidates for fusion blanket structural applications. Their 60-point baseline, however, is limited by irradiation-induced hardening and embrittlement, resulting in a significant upward shift of the Ductile-to-Brittle Transition Temperature (DBTT). At 15 dpa (the expected end-of-life dose for a DEMO blanket), the DBTT shift of ~150-200°C renders the steel brittle at operating temperatures, posing a structural integrity risk. The current baseline attempts to mitigate this via alloying modifications (e.g., adding Ta, W, and V), but these only partially slow the embrittlement. This roadmap proposes a **nanostructured RAFM steel with a high-density, stable oxide dispersion** (e.g., Y₂O₃) to act as point-defect sinks. This suppresses the formation of large dislocation loops and voids that drive the embrittlement. The target is to suppress the DBTT shift to <50°C at 15 dpa, enabling a safe, long-life blanket.

**1. Pain Point Definition (Why)**
- **Neutron-Induced Displacement Damage:** High-energy neutrons displace atoms, creating a cascade of vacancies and interstitials. These point defects cluster and form dislocation loops and voids.
- **Hardening and Embrittlement:** The loops and voids impede dislocation motion, causing hardening. More critically, they reduce the material's toughness and increase the DBTT, making it susceptible to brittle fracture.
- **Helium Embrittlement:** The (n, α) reactions on elements like B and Ni produce helium, which accumulates in the grain boundaries, further embrittling the steel.

**2. The 60-Point Ceiling (Old Route)**
The 60-point baseline uses conventional RAFM steels with a small number of precipitates (e.g., TaC, VN) that provide modest radiation resistance. However, these precipitates are not stable at high dpa and coarsen over time, reducing their effectiveness. At 15 dpa, the DBTT shift is unacceptably high (150-200°C). *The 60-point ceiling has exhausted all headroom for parameter tuning – further adjustments only reduce efficiency, and any significant improvement requires a fundamental hardware change.*

**3. The Breakthrough Solution (What)**
**Core Concept:** Oxide Dispersion-Strengthened (ODS) RAFM Steel. Dispersing a high density (10²³-10²⁴ m⁻³) of fine (2-5 nm) Y₂O₃ nanoparticles throughout the steel matrix. These particles act as:
1.  **Neutron Scattering Centers:** They slightly scatter neutrons, reducing the damage rate.
2.  **Point Defect Sinks:** They absorb vacancies and interstitials, preventing them from clustering into large, embrittling dislocation loops.
3.  **Grain Boundary Pinners:** They stabilize the martensitic microstructure and prevent grain growth, which would otherwise soften the material and reduce the oxide particle density.
The manufacturing process is a mechanical alloying and hot isostatic pressing (HIP) route, which is a standard industrial technique.

**Parameter Benchmarking:**
- **DBTT Shift at 15 dpa:** Baseline +150-200°C → Target <+50°C.
- **Tensile Strength (RT):** Baseline 650 MPa → Target 700 MPa.
- **Creep Rupture Strength (550°C):** Baseline 200 MPa → Target 250 MPa.
- **Uniform Elongation:** Baseline 12% → Target 12% (no loss of ductility).

**Supply Chain Anchoring (Standard-Driven):**
- **Steel Base Material:** Need a Fe-9Cr-2W-0.2V-0.07Ta (or similar) reduced activation steel. Standard: ASTM A335 for similar compositions.
- **Oxide Dispersion:** Need Y₂O₃ (or a mixture of Y₂O₃ + HfO₂) with a particle size of <50 nm. Standard: ISO 1080 or equivalent.
- **Manufacturing:** Need Mechanical Alloying (MA) + Hot Isostatic Pressing (HIP). Standard: ASTM A988 for HIP processing.

**4. Implementation Path (How)**
- **Step A:** Develop and characterize the ODS RAFM steel at the lab scale. *Acceptance Criteria:* Demonstrate a high density (10²³ m⁻³) of stable, fine (<5 nm) Y₂O₃ particles in the as-HIPed state.
- **Step B:** Perform small-scale irradiation experiments at a fast reactor or accelerator-based neutron source. *Acceptance Criteria:* Achieve a DBTT shift of <50°C at 15 dpa.
- **Step C:** Scale up to a full-scale production process (MA + HIP) for a blanket component. *Acceptance Criteria:* Achieve mechanical properties and microstructure uniformity (within ±10%) across a 1-tonne HIP billet.

By using a high density of stable oxide particles, it creates a huge number of defect sinks, greatly suppressing the formation of large dislocation loops and voids.
