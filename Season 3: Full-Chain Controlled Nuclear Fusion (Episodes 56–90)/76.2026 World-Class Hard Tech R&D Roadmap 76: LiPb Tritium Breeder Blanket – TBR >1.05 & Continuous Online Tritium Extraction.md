**Abstract**
The LiPb (lead-lithium eutectic) breeder blanket is the most promising candidate for tritium self-sufficiency in fusion reactors, but its 60-point baseline is trapped by a fundamental conflict: achieving a high Tritium Breeding Ratio (TBR >1.05) requires a high ⁶Li enrichment and a thick blanket, which increases the tritium inventory and makes online extraction difficult. The current baseline uses a "batch processing" approach for tritium extraction, which leads to large tritium inventories, high permeation losses, and a low overall fuel cycle efficiency. This roadmap proposes a **continuous, low-inventory, vacuum-permeation-based tritium extraction system** coupled with an optimized blanket design. By integrating a thin, porous ceramic membrane (e.g., Nb or V-alloy) directly into the blanket flow channel and using a counter-current helium purge gas, we target an online extraction efficiency >90%, a blanket tritium inventory <100 g, and a TBR of 1.1.

**1. Pain Point Definition (Why)**
- **Low TBR:** Current designs often struggle to reach TBR >1.05 due to neutron leakage and parasitic absorption in structural materials. The need to extract tritium forces the use of lower ⁶Li enrichments or thinner blankets, further reducing the TBR.
- **High Tritium Inventory:** The conventional approach relies on batch extraction (e.g., periodic vacuum degassing or gas-liquid contactors). This leads to a large tritium inventory within the blanket itself, increasing the risk of permeation, radioactive release, and operational cost.
- **Low Online Extraction Efficiency:** Batch systems cannot continuously remove tritium, leading to a build-up of tritium in the LiPb, which increases the chemical activity and the driving force for permeation into the coolant or the environment.

**2. The 60-Point Ceiling (Old Route)**
The 60-point baseline uses a "batch" extraction system. To achieve a high TBR, the ⁶Li enrichment is maximized, but this increases the tritium generation rate, which the batch extraction system cannot keep up with. This leads to high inventories and a "leaky" blanket. *The 60-point ceiling has exhausted all headroom for parameter tuning – further adjustments only reduce efficiency, and any significant improvement requires a fundamental hardware change.*

**3. The Breakthrough Solution (What)**
**Core Concept:** Continuous Vacuum-Permeation Membrane Extraction. A thin (10-50 µm) metallic membrane (e.g., Nb, V, or Pd-coated) is placed in the LiPb flow channel. The tritium (as T₂ or T⁺ in LiPb) diffuses through the membrane due to the chemical potential gradient. On the permeate side, a sweep gas (e.g., He + 0.1% H₂) carries the tritium to a downstream storage or purification system. This system is continuous and has a very low tritium inventory. The blanket is optimized for TBR by using a "flow-through" design with a high ⁶Li enrichment (90%) and a thick (1.5 m) breeder zone.

**Parameter Benchmarking:**
- **Tritium Breeding Ratio (TBR):** Baseline 1.05 → Target 1.10.
- **Tritium Inventory (Blanket):** Baseline >1 kg → Target <100 g.
- **Online Extraction Efficiency:** Baseline <50% → Target >95%.
- **Permeation Loss:** Baseline 10-20% → Target <1%.

**Supply Chain Anchoring (Standard-Driven):**
- **LiPb Eutectic:** Requires LiPb (17 at% Li, 83 at% Pb), with ⁶Li enrichment. Standard: LiPb alloy, sourced from standard Li-metal and Pb production.
- **Membrane Material:** Requires a hydrogen-permeable metallic membrane. Standard: Niobium (Nb) or Vanadium (V) foil (ASTM B393-18 or B708-18).
- **Sweep Gas:** He with 0.1-1% H₂. Standard: UHP (Ultra High Purity) He and H₂.

**4. Implementation Path (How)**
- **Step A:** Develop a lab-scale experiment with a small LiPb loop and a membrane permeator. *Acceptance Criteria:* Achieve a tritium permeation flux of >1 mol·m⁻²·s⁻¹ at 500°C, with a permeate-side recovery efficiency of >90%.
- **Step B:** Integrate the membrane concept into a 1/10th scale blanket section with a neutron source (e.g., a DT neutron generator). *Acceptance Criteria:* Demonstrate a TBR of >1.05 in an integrated experiment.
- **Step C:** Design and construct a full-scale prototype. *Acceptance Criteria:* A full-scale blanket module that can achieve a TBR of 1.1 and maintain a <100 g tritium inventory under realistic operating conditions.

**5. Isomorphic Mapping Standards (Domain-Specific Application)**
- **Physics/Engineering:** The solution relies on COTS metallic foils and standard pressure equipment. The design is robust to pressure fluctuations and temperature gradients.
- **Code/AI:** Simulation must move from a simple 1D "tritium transport" model to a 3D multi-physics model that couples neutronics, thermal-hydraulics, and tritium permeation.

**6. Final Verdict**
**【Breakthrough Level】**
*Reasoning:* This solution breaks the conventional linkage between high TBR and high tritium inventory. By using a continuous permeation membrane, it decouples the TBR (determined by blanket thickness and enrichment) from the tritium removal rate (determined by the membrane surface area). This provides a path to a truly tritium self-sufficient fusion reactor with a safe, low-inventory fuel cycle.
