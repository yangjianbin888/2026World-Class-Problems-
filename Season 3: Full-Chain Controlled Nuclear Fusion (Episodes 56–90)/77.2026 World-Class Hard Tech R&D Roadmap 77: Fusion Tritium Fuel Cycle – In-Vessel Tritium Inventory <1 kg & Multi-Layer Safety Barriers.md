**Abstract**
The tritium fuel cycle is the Achilles' heel of fusion energy. The 60-point baseline operates with a total in-vessel and plant inventory of 2-5 kg, which is both economically crippling (tritium costs ~$30,000/g) and a radiological hazard. The high inventory stems from a "keep it all in the system" philosophy, relying on large storage beds and batch processing, which leads to large tritium holdup in the blanket, the plasma exhaust, and the isotope separation system. This roadmap proposes a **"Just-in-Time" (JIT) fueling and extraction system** based on three principles: (1) **Continuous extraction** (see roadmap #76 for the blanket-level extraction), (2) **Low-inventory storage** using metal hydride beds with fast kinetics, and (3) **Passive safety barriers** that rely on physical containment rather than active control. The target is to reduce the total in-vessel tritium inventory to <1 kg while maintaining a TBR >1.05, thereby making fusion economically viable and environmentally acceptable.

**1. Pain Point Definition (Why)**
- **High Inventory:** The conventional "batch" fuel cycle requires large tritium storage beds to buffer the mismatch between continuous fuel burn and periodic extraction. This leads to inventories of >2 kg just in the storage beds.
- **Slow Extraction:** The extraction from the blanket and the plasma exhaust is slow, leading to a large "in-process" inventory in the piping and the vacuum pumps.
- **Inadequate Safety Barriers:** The current safety approach relies on active control systems and containment buildings, rather than inherent, passive barriers. This is a risk for a high-inventory, high-hazard facility.

**2. The 60-Point Ceiling (Old Route)**
The 60-point baseline uses a "batch" fuel cycle. It stores tritium in large metal hydride beds (e.g., LaNi₄.₇Al₀.₃) and then, in a periodic cycle, extracts it for injection. This approach guarantees a large inventory, high permeation losses, and a complex, failure-prone safety system. *The 60-point ceiling has exhausted all headroom for parameter tuning – further adjustments only reduce efficiency, and any significant improvement requires a fundamental hardware change.*

**3. The Breakthrough Solution (What)**
**Core Concept:** A Just-in-Time, continuous, low-inventory fuel cycle with three key subsystems:
1.  **Blanket:** Continuous vacuum-permeation extraction (from #76) to remove tritium as it is bred, keeping the blanket inventory <100 g.
2.  **Fuel Processing:** A "direct internal recycle" (DIR) system that bypasses the large isotope separation columns. The unburned tritium and deuterium from the plasma exhaust are separated via a small, fast-response, cryogenic molecular sieve, reducing the processing time and inventory.
3.  **Storage:** A "fast-kinetics" metal hydride bed (e.g., TiFe-based) that can release tritium on demand in seconds, rather than hours, minimizing the stored inventory.
4.  **Safety Barriers:** A multi-layer passive containment (e.g., double-wall piping, passively cooled catalytic recombiners, and a vacuum vessel that is inherently leak-tight).

**Parameter Benchmarking:**
- **Total In-Vessel Inventory:** Baseline 2-5 kg → Target <1 kg.
- **Fuel Cycle Residence Time:** Baseline >6 hours → Target <1 hour.
- **Tritium Permeation Loss:** Baseline 10-20% → Target <1%.
- **Safety System Complexity:** Baseline Active (pumps, valves, control logic) → Target Passive (pressure, temperature, geometry).

**Supply Chain Anchoring (Standard-Driven):**
- **Metal Hydride Beds:** TiFe-based or LaNi₅-based hydride. Standard: ASTM B928-19.
- **Cryogenic Molecular Sieve:** Zeolite 5A or 13X, with a pore size of 5-10 Å. Standard: ASTM D2854.
- **Piping:** Double-walled 316L stainless steel with Helium leak detection. Standard: ASME B31.3.

**4. Implementation Path (How)**
- **Step A:** Develop a "fast-kinetics" metal hydride bed that can absorb/desorb tritium in <60 seconds. *Acceptance Criteria:* A bed that can cycle between 10% and 90% of its capacity in <60 seconds at a temperature of 300°C.
- **Step B:** Integrate the DIR system with a small, fast-response cryogenic molecular sieve into the fuel processing line. *Acceptance Criteria:* Achieve a H₂/DT separation factor of >100 and a processing time of <5 minutes.
- **Step C:** Design and construct a full-scale prototype of the JIT fuel cycle. *Acceptance Criteria:* Demonstrate a total in-vessel tritium inventory of <1 kg, with the fuel cycle being able to process the full 100% of the D-T burn rate, while maintaining a passive safety system.
