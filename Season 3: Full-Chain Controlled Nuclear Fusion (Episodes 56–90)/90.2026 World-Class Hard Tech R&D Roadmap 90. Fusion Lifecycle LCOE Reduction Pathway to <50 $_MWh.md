**Abstract**
This roadmap addresses the core challenge of achieving a lifecycle Levelized Cost of Electricity (LCOE) below 50 $/MWh for commercial fusion power plants, considered the threshold for broad grid competitiveness against solar, wind, and gas. The current 60-point baseline is defined by first-of-a-kind (FOAK) fusion plant conceptual designs with projected LCOE in the 80-150 $/MWh range, characterized by high capital costs ($5-15B), long construction timelines (>10 years), and a physics-first design philosophy. This 90-point solution integrates three synergistic strategies: (1) **Design-for-Cost and Modularization**: anchoring on factory-fabricated, standardized components and modular plant architectures to slash capital costs and construction timelines, reusing existing power plant infrastructure where possible; (2) **Integrated Thermal Storage (ITS)**: decoupling the fusion core's continuous operation from intermittent grid price signals to maximize revenue and effective capacity factor; and (3) **System-Level Economic Optimization**: using validated costing frameworks (e.g., pyFECONs, GEM) to treat LCOE as a primary design constraint from the outset, identifying and attacking dominant cost drivers (magnets, lasers, balance-of-plant, indirect costs) through targeted R&D and supply chain development. The approach leverages commercially available off-the-shelf (COTS) industrial components where possible and is validated by recent techno-economic analyses from ARPA-E, LLNL, and industry sources.

**1. Pain Point Definition (Why)**
The 60-point baseline for fusion LCOE is defined by the traditional physics-first design paradigm, where economic viability is considered only after scientific breakeven (Q>1) is achieved. This manifests in three critical failure modes:
- **Failure Mode 1: Physics-First Cost Escalation.** Designing for maximum performance (e.g., highest magnetic field, largest plasma volume, highest gain) without concurrent cost optimization leads to exponentially increasing capital expenditures. ARIES-style cost-scaling studies show that for many fusion concepts, the indirect and balance-of-plant costs, not the fusion core itself, dominate total plant cost. This creates a "physics trap" where chasing performance metrics drives costs beyond commercial viability.
- **Failure Mode 2: Long, Costly Construction Timelines.** The traditional approach of on-site, custom engineering and construction for each fusion plant, akin to large fission reactors, leads to construction timelines of 10-15 years. During this period, interest accumulates, and market conditions change, making the project economically uncompetitive against solar farms that can be deployed in <2 years.
- **Failure Mode 3: Misalignment with Grid Price Signals.** Fusion's greatest advantage is steady, 24/7 baseload power. However, in grids with high renewables penetration, wholesale electricity prices can drop to 0 $/MWh during peak solar/wind hours. A fusion plant selling power at these times loses revenue. Without thermal storage, it is forced to sell into low-price periods, making it difficult to achieve a competitive LCOE despite its high capacity factor.

**Old Route Ceiling (60-point Baseline)**
The 60-point baseline for fusion LCOE is characterized by FOAK designs with capital costs exceeding $5B and an LCOE >80 $/MWh. The old route's 60 points have used up all the physics parameter degrees of freedom; any further improvement in plasma performance leads to higher costs due to the need for larger, more expensive ancillary systems. Its limit is not a technological limitation; it is an economic and system-design one.

**2. Breakthrough Solution (What)**
**Core Architecture:** A fusion power plant designed from the outset with LCOE < 50 $/MWh as the primary constraint, achieved through:
1.  **Cost-Driven Design & Modularization:** Implementing design-for-cost practices, factory fabrication, and modular construction to reduce capital costs and construction timelines by >40%. Retrofitting existing coal plant sites can save ~30% of balance-of-plant costs.
2.  **Integrated Thermal Storage (ITS):** Including a low-cost thermal storage system (e.g., molten salt) that allows the fusion core to run continuously while dispatching electricity only during high-price periods. Modeling suggests this can improve effective LCOE even when selling power for only 12 hours a day.
3.  **Supply Chain Development:** Building domestic and allied supply chains for critical components (superconducting magnets, high-voltage capacitors, specialized materials) before China's lead becomes insurmountable, locking in cost and security advantages.

**Parametric Benchmark:**
- **LCOE Target:** Baseline: 80-150 $/MWh (FOAK projection) → **Solution: <50 $/MWh**, the threshold for broad competitiveness.
- **Capital Cost (Overnight):** Baseline: >$5B → **Solution: <$2B** (target) achieved through modularization and design-for-cost.
- **Construction Timeline:** Baseline: 10-15 years → **Solution: <5 years** (first core components on site) through modularization and off-site fabrication.
- **Effective Capacity Factor (with ITS):** Baseline: 90% (continuous operation) → **Solution: 90% (core) / >50% (grid dispatch)** , maximizing revenue by selling only at peak prices.
- **LCOE Reduction Levers:** **40-50% from cost-out** (modularization, supply chain, BOP reuse) and **20-30% from revenue optimization** (ITS, cogeneration, capacity payments).

**Supply Chain Anchoring (COTS Focus):**
- **Modular Balance-of-Plant (BOP):** **Standard:** Factory-fabricated, standardized BOP modules (turbines, heat exchangers, cooling towers) that can be shipped in containers and assembled on-site. **Performance Standard:** Must be capable of rapid deployment and mass production to achieve cost reductions via learning.
- **Thermal Storage System:** **Standard:** Molten salt thermal storage, a mature technology used in concentrated solar power (CSP) plants. **Performance Standard:** Must be capable of storing thermal energy from the fusion core for >12 hours with minimal losses.
- **Critical Components:** **Standard:** High-voltage capacitors, superconducting magnets, and specialized materials. **Performance Standard:** Must meet fusion-specific durability and performance standards while being sourced from domestic or allied suppliers to ensure supply chain security.

**3. Implementation Path (How)**

- **Action:** Implement a design-for-cost philosophy, using the open-source pyFECONs costing framework or LLNL's GEM model to evaluate design trade-offs in real-time. Design the plant as a set of modular, factory-fabricated "plug-and-play" sub-systems that can be rapidly assembled on-site. Conduct a supply chain risk assessment for critical components and identify domestic or allied suppliers to avoid single-source or overseas dependencies.
- **Acceptance Criteria (Quantified):**
    - **Achieve a conceptual design with an overnight capital cost < $2B**, as estimated by pyFECONs.
    - **Identify modularization pathways** that can reduce on-site construction labor by >40%.
    - **Develop a supply chain map** with at least two qualified suppliers for each critical component within the U.S. or allied nations.

**Step B: Integrate Thermal Storage and Revenue Optimization.**
- **Action:** Incorporate a thermal storage system (e.g., molten salt) into the plant design. Size the storage to allow the plant to sell power only during peak grid price hours (e.g., 12-16 hours/day) while the fusion core operates continuously. Model the revenue stream using real-time grid price data to ensure that the ITS improves the project's overall economics.
- **Acceptance Criteria (Quantified):**
    - **Design a thermal storage system** capable of storing >60% of the plant's thermal output for >8 hours.
    - **Demonstrate, via modeling, that ITS allows the plant to sell >80% of its output at prices >80 $/MWh**, effectively reducing the LCOE to <50 $/MWh even if the core's cost is higher.

**Step C: Validate Lifecycle Economics and Cost Reduction.**
- **Action:** Perform a full lifecycle cost analysis, including construction, operation, maintenance, fuel (tritium), and decommissioning. Use the pyFECONs framework to provide a transparent, auditable, and standardized LCOE estimate. Validate the cost reduction pathways against independent benchmarks (e.g., ARPA-E, DOE).
- **Acceptance Criteria (Quantified):**
    - **Achieve a lifecycle LCOE < 50 $/MWh** for an Nth-of-a-kind (NOAK) plant, as calculated by the validated costing framework.
    - **Demonstrate a learning curve** that shows the LCOE dropping below the threshold by the 3rd-5th plant, confirming the scalability of the cost-out strategies.
    - **Show that the plant can achieve a 10-15% rate of return** without subsidies, proving its commercial viability.

**8. Preemptive Q&A**
- **Q:** Is $50/MWh really the "magic number" for fusion?
    → **A:** It is the widely recognized threshold for broad grid competitiveness against solar, wind, and gas in most markets. While some high-price markets (e.g., Singapore, Japan) can support higher LCOEs, reaching $50/MWh unlocks the global market.
- **Q:** Can fusion really achieve a construction timeline of <5 years?
    → **A:** Yes, but only with a modular, factory-fabricated design philosophy that mirrors the approach used in the gas turbine industry. On-site work would be primarily assembly and integration, not custom engineering.

    → **A:** It improves revenue, not just cost. By allowing the plant to sell power only when prices are high (e.g., evening peak, nighttime), it increases the effective value of each MWh sold, making the higher capital cost bearable. The storage itself is a relatively low-cost addition to a fusion plant.

- **失效模式1：物理优先导致成本失控。** 在不进行并行成本优化的情况下追求最高性能（如最高磁场、最大等离子体体积、最高增益）会导致资本支出呈指数级增长。ARIES风格的成本定标研究表明，对于许多聚变概念，间接成本和辅助设施成本（而非聚变堆芯本身）主导了总电厂成本。这形成了一个“物理陷阱”——追求性能指标驱动成本超出商业可行范围。
- **失效模式2：长建设周期和高成本。** 传统方法对每个聚变电站采用现场定制工程和建造，类似于大型裂变堆，导致建设周期长达10-15年。在此期间，利息累积且市场条件变化，使项目在不到2年即可部署的太阳能发电场面前失去经济竞争力。
- **失效模式3：与电网价格信号脱节。** 聚变的最大优势是稳定的24/7基载电力。然而，在可再生能源高渗透率的电网中，批发电价在太阳能/风能高峰时段可能降至0 $/MWh。在这些时段售电的聚变电站将损失收入。若无热储能，它被迫在低价时段售电，使其难以实现有竞争力的LCOE，尽管容量因子很高。

**Zusammenfassung**
Diese Roadmap adressiert die Kernherausforderung, die Lebenszyklus-Stromgestehungskosten (LCOE) kommerzieller Fusionskraftwerke auf unter 50 $/MWh zu senken – eine Schwelle, die als entscheidend für die Wettbewerbsfähigkeit von Fusionsenergie gegenüber Solar-, Wind- und Gaskraftwerken auf dem Strommarkt gilt. Die aktuelle 60-Punkte-Basislinie wird durch Konzeptdesigns von Erstanlagen (FOAK) mit prognostizierten LCOE im Bereich von 80-150 $/MWh definiert, gekennzeichnet durch hohe Kapitalkosten (5-15 Mrd. $), lange Bauzeiten (>10 Jahre) und eine "Physik-zuerst"-Designphilosophie. Diese 90-Punkte-Lösung integriert drei synergistische Strategien: (1) **Kostengesteuertes Design und Modularisierung**: Verankerung auf fabrikgefertigten, standardisierten Komponenten und modularen Kraftwerksarchitekturen, um Kapitalkosten und Bauzeiten drastisch zu senken, mit Wiederverwendung bestehender Kraftwerksinfrastruktur wo möglich; (2) **Integrierte thermische Speicherung (ITS)** : Entkopplung des kontinuierlichen Betriebs des Fusionskerns von intermittierenden Strompreissignalen zur Maximierung von Umsatz und effektivem Kapazitätsfaktor; und (3) **Systemweite wirtschaftliche Optimierung**: Verwendung validierter Kostenrahmen (z.B. pyFECONs, GEM), um LCOE von Anfang an als primäre Designbeschränkung zu behandeln, dominante Kostentreiber (Magnete, Laser, Hilfsanlagen, indirekte Kosten) durch gezielte F&E und Lieferkettenentwicklung zu identifizieren und zu reduzieren. Der Ansatz nutzt wo möglich kommerziell erhältliche Standardkomponenten und wird durch aktuelle techno-ökonomische Analysen von ARPA-E, LLNL und aus der Industrie validiert.

**1. Schmerzpunkt-Definition**
Die 60-Punkte-Basislinie für Fusions-LCOE wird durch das traditionelle "Physik-zuerst"-Designparadigma definiert, bei dem die wirtschaftliche Tragfähigkeit erst nach Erreichen des wissenschaftlichen Breakeven (Q>1) in Betracht gezogen wird. Dies äußert sich in drei kritischen Versagensmodi:
- **Versagensmodus 1: Physik-zuerst-Kosteneskalation.** Das Design für maximale Leistung (z.B. höchstes Magnetfeld, größtes Plasmavolumen, höchster Gain) ohne gleichzeitige Kostenoptimierung führt zu exponentiell steigenden Investitionsausgaben. ARIES-Studien zur Kostenskalierung zeigen, dass für viele Fusionskonzepte die indirekten Kosten und Hilfsanlagenkosten, nicht der Fusionskern selbst, die Gesamtkosten dominieren. Dies erzeugt eine "Physikfalle", bei der das Streben nach Leistungskennzahlen die Kosten über die wirtschaftliche Tragfähigkeit hinaus treibt.
- **Versagensmodus 2: Lange, kostspielige Bauzeiten.** Der traditionelle Ansatz der Vor-Ort-Kundentechnik und -Konstruktion für jedes Fusionskraftwerk, ähnlich wie bei großen Spaltungsreaktoren, führt zu Bauzeiten von 10-15 Jahren. Während dieser Zeit fallen Zinsen an und die Marktbedingungen ändern sich, was das Projekt wirtschaftlich gegenüber Solarparks, die in <2 Jahren deployed werden können, unkonkurrenzfähig macht.
- **Versagensmodus 3: Fehlausrichtung mit Strompreissignalen.** Der größte Vorteil der Fusion ist die stetige 24/7-Grundlast. In Netzen mit hohem Anteil erneuerbarer Energien können die Großhandelsstrompreise jedoch während der Spitzen-Solar-/Wind-Stunden auf 0 $/MWh fallen. Ein Fusionskraftwerk, das zu diesen Zeiten Strom verkauft, verliert Einnahmen. Ohne Wärmespeicher ist es gezwungen, in Niedrigpreisperioden zu verkaufen, was es schwierig macht, einen wettbewerbsfähigen LCOE zu erreichen, trotz seines hohen Kapazitätsfaktors.

**Decke des alten Weges (60-Punkte-Basislinie)**
Die 60-Punkte-Basislinie für den Fusions-LCOE ist durch FOAK-Designs mit Kapitalkosten über 5 Mrd. $ und einem LCOE >80 $/MWh gekennzeichnet. Die 60 Punkte des alten Weges haben alle physikalischen Parameter-Freiheitsgrade ausgeschöpft; jede weitere Verbesserung der Plasmaperformance führt zu höheren Kosten aufgrund der Notwendigkeit größerer, teurerer Hilfssysteme. Seine Grenze ist keine technologische Einschränkung, sondern eine wirtschaftliche und systemische Designbeschränkung.

**Kernarchitektur:** Ein Fusionskraftwerk, das von Anfang an mit LCOE < 50 $/MWh als primärer Beschränkung ausgelegt ist, erreicht durch:

3.  **Lieferkettenentwicklung:** Aufbau inländischer und verbündeter Lieferketten für kritische Komponenten (supraleitende Magnete, Hochspannungskondensatoren, Spezialmaterialien), bevor Chinas Vorsprung unüberwindbar wird, um Kosten- und Sicherheitsvorteile zu sichern.
